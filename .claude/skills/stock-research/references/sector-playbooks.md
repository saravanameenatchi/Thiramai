# Sector Playbooks — what actually matters per sector

Use with `stock-research` Rule 5. For each sector: **key drivers**, **metrics**, **red flags**, and
**what the textbook gets wrong**.

---

## AI value chain (map the layer before valuing)
| Layer | Examples of the type | Watch |
|---|---|---|
| Accelerators / GPUs / custom ASICs | GPU makers, custom-silicon designers | Hyperscaler capex guidance, software ecosystem lock-in, custom-chip substitution, export rules |
| Foundry & advanced packaging | Leading-edge foundry | Node leadership, packaging capacity, pricing power, geographic risk |
| Memory (HBM/DRAM/NAND) | Memory makers | Supply discipline, HBM share and qualification, capex/sales, contract pricing |
| Networking / optics / interconnect | Switching, optical modules | Cluster scale-out, attach rate per GPU |
| Power, grid, cooling | Utilities, electrical equipment, thermal | Interconnect queues, backlog, pricing, multi-year contracts |
| Data centers / cloud | Hyperscalers, neoclouds | Capex ÷ operating cash flow, depreciation life, AI revenue disclosed, debt funding |
| Models / platforms | Frontier labs (mostly private) | Revenue run-rate, compute cost per revenue, enterprise share |
| Applications / software | SaaS, vertical AI | Seat disruption vs usage pricing, gross margin under inference costs |
| AI adopters (any sector) | Banks, staples, industrials | Measurable opex savings, margin expansion attributable to automation |

**AI-specific tests**
- *Who is paying, and with what?* Customer capex funded from operating cash flow = healthy; funded by debt, SPVs, or vendor financing = fragile.
- *Depreciation reality:* are GPU useful-life assumptions (5–6 yrs) realistic vs product cadence (~1–2 yrs)?
- *Gross margin under inference costs* for software claiming AI revenue.
- *Customer concentration:* top customers > 40% of revenue = discount the multiple.

## Semiconductors (non-memory)
- Metrics: EV/EBIT vs 2-yr growth, gross-margin trajectory, R&D intensity, design-win pipeline, book-to-bill, inventory days (own + distributor).
- Red flags: rising channel inventory, double-ordering, lengthening receivable days, pull-forward ahead of tariffs.
- Textbook miss: trailing P/E is meaningless at inflections; use next-cycle earnings.

## Memory
- Buy when P/B is near cycle lows, capex is being cut, and inventories are clearing — usually when the P/E looks *high* or negative.
- Trim when P/E is lowest, industry capex is rising, and spot prices roll over below contract.
- HBM may dampen the cycle (qualification moat, long-term agreements) — needs contract evidence.

## Software / internet
- Rule of 40 (growth % + FCF margin %), net revenue retention, gross retention, CAC payback, SBC-adjusted FCF, EV/GP ÷ growth.
- AI lens: per-seat pricing at risk if agents replace seats; usage/consumption pricing benefits.
- Red flags: decelerating NRR, rising SBC, deferred revenue slowing faster than revenue.

## Healthcare / pharma / biotech
- Risk-adjusted pipeline NPV (phase probabilities), patent-cliff timeline (% of revenue losing exclusivity in 5 yrs), manufacturing capacity, payer coverage, pricing policy exposure.
- Leader test: superior clinical data + capacity + next-generation pipeline + cash to fund trials.
- Red flags: single-asset concentration, safety signals, compounding/generic erosion, policy price resets.

## Consumer staples (defensive)
- **Volume vs price/mix split** of organic growth — price-only growth is not durable.
- Gross margin vs input costs, FCF conversion (>90% is good), dividend payout and coverage, net debt/EBITDA, emerging-markets exposure, private-label share trend.
- Contemporary drivers: GLP-1 drugs reducing calorie consumption (snacks, beverages, alcohol), retailer power and private label, tariffs, AI-driven SG&A savings and demand forecasting.
- Undervaluation signal: market prices "no growth forever," but volumes are stabilizing and the company has a portfolio-reshaping catalyst (divestitures, M&A, cost programme).
- Textbook miss: "staples are safe" — a 20x P/E staple with negative volume is a bond with equity risk.

## Financials
- Banks: P/TBV vs ROTE, NIM sensitivity, deposit beta, credit-cost normalization, CET1.
- Insurers: combined ratio, reserve development, investment yield.
- Exchanges / data / payments: treat like quality compounders (network effects).

## Industrials
- Backlog and book-to-bill, aftermarket/service % of revenue (recurring), pricing vs cost, exposure to AI data-center power build-out and reshoring.

## Energy / materials
- Position on the cost curve, FCF yield at the futures strip, reserve life, capital discipline, shareholder returns.

## Utilities / REITs
- Rate-base growth, allowed ROE, regulatory relations, AFFO yield, debt maturity profile; data-center power demand can turn a utility into a growth stock — verify signed contracts.
