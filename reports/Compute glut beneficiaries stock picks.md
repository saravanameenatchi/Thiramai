# Cheap tokens, not idle GPUs, crown winners

The thesis is half right as of November 2026. "Monetizers will win" holds up as a direction: the price of a fixed level of AI capability is falling 5-10x or more a year, and that deflation already favors companies that sell outcomes rather than compute. But there is **no hardware glut yet**. NVIDIA just grew revenue 103% year over year, H100 rental rates *rose* in 2026, and North American data-center vacancy sits near 1%. Today's "glut" is in intelligence per dollar, not in idle GPUs. The claim that healthcare, robotics and defense are the *biggest* beneficiaries is not supported by revenue data. Coding agents and enterprise assistants account for 76% of enterprise AI application spend, and the frontier labs capture most of it. Healthcare is the largest vertical, but at **$3.7B it is about a ninth the size of coding**. Robotics and defense show up mainly as record venture flows and government budget requests, not as AI revenue. Among the three sectors, the investable winners are businesses where cheap inference widens margins on something already paid for: reimbursed diagnostics (Tempus, HeartFlow), systems of record (Pro Medicus, Palantir), scaled operators and component suppliers (Intuitive Surgical, Symbotic, Hesai), and backlog-rich defense manufacturers (Rheinmetall, Saab, Karman). Humanoid makers and small drone makers are where the hype outruns the evidence. **Current market capitalizations and valuation multiples were unavailable for nearly every name in this report**, so the tiers below rank business evidence, not price. You need live quotes before acting on any of them.

## Compute sellers still dominate; the glut so far is in price per token

Every hard indicator of physical oversupply points the other way. NVIDIA's quarter ended October 25, 2026 brought **$115.8B of revenue (+103% YoY)**, of which data center was $107.6B (+110%). Guidance for the next quarter is $130B ([NVIDIA](https://nvidianews.nvidia.com/news/nvidia-announces-financial-results-for-third-quarter-fiscal-2027)). The one crack is a guided 3-4 point step-down in gross margin to 71-72%. That could be early pricing pressure, or it could simply reflect the Vera Rubin ramp; the release does not say which. The Big Four hyperscalers have guided to roughly **$720-745B of 2026 capex**, and Alphabet's CFO expects capex to "increase significantly in 2027" ([datacentres.com](https://www.datacentres.com/guides/hyperscaler-capex-trends)). Microsoft's trim was caused by delayed third-party *supply*, not weak demand ([valueaddvc](https://valueaddvc.com/blog/big-tech-ai-capex-in-2025-microsoft-google-meta-amazon-and-the-spending-race)). The SemiAnalysis H100 one-year contract index climbed from $1.70 to $2.35 per GPU-hour between October 2025 and March 2026 ([akash.network](https://akash.network/the-bid/h100-rental-price-2026-cost-per-hour/)). Vendor forecasts of $1.00-1.50 spot pricing by late 2026 have not materialized. Power is the main governor: up to half of 2026 data-center projects face delays, and large sites take 3-7 years to develop ([The Register](https://www.theregister.com/2026/01/14/ai_investment/)).

The real glut is in unit prices. Epoch AI measured the price of a fixed capability level falling a median **50x per year**, and 200x per year on post-2024 data ([Epoch AI](https://epoch.ai/data-insights/llm-inference-price-trends)). Menlo Ventures reports open-source token prices fell 3.3x to $0.13 per million in 2026, while blended closed-model prices fell 24% ([Menlo Ventures](https://menlovc.com/perspective/2026-the-state-of-enterprise-ai/)). There is an important caveat: the cost of running *frontier* models rose 3-18x a year ([arXiv](https://arxiv.org/html/2511.23455v2)), and reasoning agents burn far more tokens per task. "Cheap compute" therefore helps companies that can do valuable work with last year's capability level far more than companies that need the frontier.

Where stress exists, it sits in financing, not in utilization. CoreWeave grew revenue 168% to $3.66B and holds a $132.5B backlog, but 74% of that backlog lands beyond 24 months. It carries about $47B of debt, its quarterly interest expense ($846M) exceeded operating income, and some of its bonds yield above 16% ([implicator.ai](https://www.implicator.ai/coreweave-reports-q3-revenue-up-168-yoy-3-66b-vs-3-55b-est/); [tradingkey](https://www.tradingkey.com/analysis/stocks/us-stocks/262299527-coreweave-earnings-report-q3-revenue-growth-backlog-capital-expenditures-net-loss-tradingkey)). The telecom bust of 2001 is the right template here. That fiber glut was real in utilization terms: estimates of lit fiber ran at 2-5% ([iwpfinance](https://iwpfinance.com/case-studies/telecom-fiber-bubble-2000)). Leveraged builders failed even though demand was real ([syncretica](https://syncretica.substack.com/p/the-second-photonic-shock)). If a compute glut arrives, most plausibly in 2027-2029 once power constraints ease and Rubin supply lands, the first casualties are likely to be highly indebted neoclouds, not NVIDIA. GPUs depreciate in 3-6 years rather than decades, so any glut is likely to be generation-specific (cheap H100s) and short-lived.

## Coding and assistants capture the money; the three named sectors capture belief

Menlo's November 2026 survey sizes enterprise AI deployment spend at **$176.8B, up 5.4x**. Applications account for $77.3B of it. **Coding alone is $34.0B (+7.7x)**, and Claude Code plus OpenAI Codex take 70% of that. Enterprise chatbots and assistants add another $25.0B. All vertical AI combined is $10.1B, and **healthcare is the largest vertical at $3.7B (+2.5x)**. Neither robotics nor defense registers as an enterprise AI software category ([Menlo Ventures](https://menlovc.com/perspective/2026-the-state-of-enterprise-ai/)). The fiber analogy makes the same point from history: the winners after 2002 were horizontal aggregators of demand, not vertical specialists. Today's largest "monetizers of compute" are the frontier labs themselves. Anthropic reached a $65B annualized run rate by July 2026 ([valueaddvc](https://valueaddvc.com/blog/anthropic-revenue-hits-47b-run-rate-how-it-passed-openai-in-just-five-months)). Even Palantir, the defense poster child, now earns more from US commercial customers ($984M, +148%) than from the US government ([SEC 8-K](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/0001321655/000132165526000045/a2026q3ex991pressrelease.htm)).

Capital flows tell the opposite story, and that gap is the most useful signal in this research. Robotics startups raised **$18.8B by June 2026**, already above 2025's record full year ([Crunchbase News](https://news.crunchbase.com/robotics/startup-venture-funding-surges-2026-data/)). Broad-definition defense tech drew **$35.4B in H1 2026** ([PitchBook](https://pitchbook.com/news/reports/q2-2026-defense-tech-vc-first-look)), after $49.1B in all of 2025 ([Defense News](https://www.defensenews.com/industry/2026/01/20/defense-tech-startups-had-their-best-funding-year-ever-in-2025/)). US digital health, which has the most measurable AI revenue of the three, raised only **$11.1B through Q3** ([Rock Health](https://rockhealth.com/insights/q3-2026-market-update-crystallizing-fifteen-years-of-digital-health/)). Money is flowing hardest where revenue is thinnest. Skild AI's valuation tripled to more than $14B in seven months, and Saronic's more than doubled to $9.25B in a year.

## Healthcare rewards outcome-priced AI and punishes AI given away free

The progress is real and measurable. The FDA has authorized **more than 1,600 AI-enabled devices** ([FDA](https://www.fda.gov/medical-devices/digital-health-center-excellence/artificial-intelligence-enabled-medical-devices)), and about 76% of them are in radiology ([Radiology Business](https://radiologybusiness.com/topics/artificial-intelligence/radiology-gets-68-new-fda-cleared-algorithms)). AI drug discovery reached a genuine milestone when Insilico moved its TNIK inhibitor rentosertib directly into Phase 3 for IPF ([Citeline Scrip](https://insights.citeline.com/scrip/focus-on-asia/china/insilico-fast-tracks-first-ai-designed-tnik-inhibitor-into-phase-iii-for-ipf-JNWTD433QFHC5IZUDCKV3JCE6U/link-DUYP5VHPIJFOPHASZNJGJ7GADM)). There is still no approved AI-discovered drug, and a 52-week trial design pushes the first readout to 2027-28 at the earliest. Investment is concentrated in platform leaders with Big Tech or Big Pharma backers: Isomorphic Labs raised a $2.1B Series B ([R&D World](https://www.rdworldonline.com/alphabet-spinoff-isomorphic-labs-raises-2-1-billion-in-quest-to-solve-all-disease-with-ai-based-drug-discovery-tools/)), and Lilly committed up to $1B with NVIDIA ([NVIDIA](https://nvidianews.nvidia.com/news/nvidia-and-lilly-announce-co-innovation-lab-to-reinvent-drug-discovery-in-the-age-of-ai)). That Lilly deal is a warning for AI-discovery vendors: when pharma builds its own AI capability, the customer becomes the competitor.

The dividing line between winners and losers is how the AI is priced. HeartFlow sells a reimbursed AI cardiac test, and its non-GAAP gross margin rose from 76.8% to 83.8% as revenue grew 57% ([HeartFlow 8-K](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/0001464521/000146452126000171/htfl-20261112xex99_1.htm)). Doximity gave its AI tools away free, and its gross margin *fell* from 93% to 91% on AI infrastructure costs with no AI revenue in guidance ([AOL transcript](https://www.aol.com/articles/doximity-docs-q3-2026-earnings-140234119.html)). Ambient scribes are being commoditized in real time. Epic shipped native AI Charting on Microsoft's Dragon technology in February 2026, and analysts call it an "existential threat" to standalone vendors ([STAT](https://www.statnews.com/2026/02/04/epic-ai-charting-ambient-scribe-abridge-microsoft/)).

## Robotics gets cheaper brains but still waits on bodies

Physical AI is generating revenue in three places: surgery, warehouses and robotaxis. Intuitive Surgical grew procedures 15% and revenue about 22% to roughly $3B in Q3 2026, although guidance was described as softer and the stock fell about 8% ([Zacks](https://www.zacks.com/stock/news/3018804/isrg-sales-jump-22-can-growth-stay-ahead-of-procedure-volumes)). Symbotic holds a **$22.5B backlog** and has turned profitable ([Nasdaq](https://www.nasdaq.com/articles/symbotic-q3-earnings-call-highlights)). Waymo's last disclosed figure was more than 500k paid rides a week ([thechargeport](https://thechargeport.com/robotaxi-tracker)), but it is not separately investable inside Alphabet. Pony.ai grew robotaxi revenue 165% with a fleet of 2,768 vehicles ([SEC 6-K](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/0001969302/000110465926098113/tm2623382d1_ex99-1.htm)). Humanoids remain early and Chinese: about **19,100 units shipped in H1 2026, more than 97% from China**, many of them going to research and demonstrations rather than productive work ([Startup Fortune](https://startupfortune.com/chinas-humanoid-robot-shipments-hit-97-of-the-world-as-tesla-stalls/)). Tesla says Optimus production has begun but has disclosed no unit volumes ([xenospectrum](https://xenospectrum.com/en/tesla-q3-2026-optimus-profit/)).

Cheap compute speeds up the model and simulation layer: NVIDIA GR00T, Gemini Robotics, Physical Intelligence's π models. The binding constraints are physical, as several 2026 results show. Aurora cut its 2026 revenue guide to about $9M after a production ramp ran six weeks late ([SEC 8-K](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/0001828108/000182810826000092/aurora26q3shareholderlet.htm)). Serve Robotics ran only 917 of its roughly 2,000 robots on an average day ([SEC 8-K](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/0001832483/000183248326000048/serv-20260930_ex991earning.htm)). Zoox's federal exemption caps it at 2,500 vehicles a year. As models become commodities, value shifts to fleets that hold proprietary real-world data, to manufacturing scale, and to component suppliers. The cleanest picks-and-shovels signal is Hesai: it shipped **23,405 robot actuation modules (+97% QoQ)**, and robotics now makes up more than half of its revenue, on top of a sixth straight profitable quarter ([Finviz PR](https://finviz.com/news/419744/hesai-group-reports-third-quarter-2026-unaudited-financial-results); [KrASIA](https://kr-asia.com/robotics-related-businesses-account-for-over-half-of-hesais-q3-2026-revenue)).

The hype marker is Unitree. It listed on the STAR Market in August with **retail oversubscription above 8,000x**, opened up 629%, and closed its first day at a market value of about $50B, roughly 5-6x its IPO valuation ([SCMP](https://www.scmp.com/tech/tech-trends/article/3364499/unitree-robotics-surges-629-us66-billion-valuation-shanghai-share-debut)). Its product is mostly low-cost G1 units going into research and education.

## Defense wins on budgets, and compute is the minor input

Defense is the strongest of the three sectors on demand, but for reasons that have little to do with a compute glut. The FY2027 US request totals about $1.5T and includes about **$53.6B for autonomy, drones and contested logistics** plus $21B for munitions and counter-drone systems ([DefenseScoop](https://defensescoop.com/2026/04/21/dod-plans-largest-ever-investment-drones-anti-drone-weapons/)). Golden Dome adds about $17.9B ([Aerospace America](https://aerospaceamerica.aiaa.org/pentagon-fiscal-27-budget-aims-to-operationalize-golden-dome/)). Much of this money depends on a second reconciliation package that Congress has not passed, so it is intent, not obligated funding. Europe's commitment to 5% of GDP by 2035 ([Janes](https://www.janes.com/osint-insights/defence-and-national-security-analysis/nato-leaders-agree-to-invest-5-of-gdp-on-defence-and-security-by-2035)) already shows up in backlogs: Rheinmetall's is €78.0B ([Rheinmetall](https://www.rheinmetall.com/en/media/news-watch/news/2026/11/2026-11-05-rheinmetall-news-quarterly-statement-q3)). Cheaper inference matters at the edge, in autonomy and sensor fusion, more than in data-center $/hr.

Program-of-record money is concentrating, and much of the best of it is private. Anduril and General Atomics won Collaborative Combat Aircraft (CCA) Increment 1 production four months ahead of schedule, beating Northrop ([Air & Space Forces](https://www.airandspaceforces.com/air-force-general-atomics-anduril-cca-production-contracts/)). Anduril raised at $61B, and Shield AI, Saronic and Helsing raised at $9-18B each ([TechCrunch](https://techcrunch.com/2026/03/26/defense-startup-shield-ai-lands-12-7b-valuation-up-140-after-u-s-air-force-deal/); [Helsing](https://helsing.ai/newsroom/helsing-raises-1-8bn-in-series-e)). The Drone Dominance program buys drones in rounds of roughly $150-300M that are re-competed each time, which produces volume without pricing power. Red Cat lost Gauntlet II, withdrew its 2026 revenue target and fell 24% ([DroneXL](https://dronexl.co/2026/11/18/red-cat-withdraws-2026-revenue-target-gauntlet-ii/)). A broader pattern ran through Q3 2026: record backlogs alongside revenue misses (Kratos, Redwire, Red Cat). Government awards are turning into revenue more slowly than bookings suggest.

## What the evidence could not verify

**Market capitalizations, valuation multiples and year-to-date performance as of November 2026 were not available for nearly every company named here.** The exceptions are Tempus (about $12.0B on October 15, 2026) and Unitree (about $50B at its August debut). That gap matters because several "quality" names, Pro Medicus and Palantir above all, have a history of premium multiples, and a great business bought at any price is not a great investment. Other unresolved items: Symbotic's fiscal Q4 and WeRide's Q3 results; Tesla Optimus volumes; any November Waymo ride count; whether the second US reconciliation bill has passed; whether SoftBank's ABB Robotics purchase has closed; Microsoft's exact 2026 capex figure; and an unverified report of a $5B Isomorphic round. Several data points come from secondary aggregators rather than filings. Robotics shipment trackers conflict with each other, and PitchBook's defense figures change materially depending on how broadly defense tech is defined.

## Conclusion

The more accurate version of the thesis is: *the next leaders will monetize deflating intelligence, and they will be companies whose prices are set by outcomes, reimbursement or budgets while their AI costs fall.* That rule cuts across sectors more cleanly than the healthcare/robotics/defense label does. It favors HeartFlow over Doximity, Palantir and Karman over Red Cat, and Hesai and Intuitive over humanoid makers trading at tens of billions on research-lab volumes. It also implies that, in 2026, the biggest beneficiaries are horizontal coding and agent platforms, mostly private labs, which none of the three named sectors can match in size.

The timing matters as much as the selection. A true hardware glut, if it comes, is a 2027-2029 event, and its first visible sign will be stress in neocloud credit (CoreWeave's bond yields and backlog conversion), not in NVIDIA's revenue. That is when the "monetize, don't sell" trade should be at its strongest, and it is the indicator to watch. Until then, the best-supported positioning is quality compounders with measurable AI-linked revenue as the core, plus small, sized-for-loss stakes in binary early-stage names (Recursion, Insilico, Pony.ai, Ondas) where a single clinical readout, fleet milestone or contract can re-rate the business. Check live valuations before buying any of them.
