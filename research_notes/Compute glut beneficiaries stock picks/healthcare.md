Research date: 2026-11-23. All figures are labeled with their reporting date. This is research, not personal financial advice. Many sources are secondary (Finviz/BusinessWire reprints, trade press). ## Q1: Real progress milestones 2024-2026 (AI drugs, FDA devices, scribes, deployments, revenue)

### Takeaway
Real, measurable progress is happening, but it is uneven. AI-enabled devices (more than 1,600 FDA authorizations, about 76% in radiology) and AI-heavy diagnostics/admin (Tempus, HeartFlow, Waystar, Pro Medicus) now show audited revenue growth. AI drug discovery has produced its first Phase 3 asset (Insilico's rentosertib) and positive Phase 2 data (Recursion REC-4881), but it has no approved drug and still depends on milestone revenue. Ambient scribes reached mass adoption in 2025-26, and in February 2026 Epic shipped a native scribe, which threatens the standalone vendors.

### Inferences
- AI drug discovery still has no approved drug, and the first real proof point (a rentosertib Phase 3 readout) is likely 2027-28 or later, given a 52-week trial design. Its value remains binary and pipeline-driven. "Cheap compute" lowers discovery cost but does not shorten clinical timelines much.
- Most of the visible monetization is in diagnostics/imaging (device-cleared, reimbursed) and in admin/RCM (ROI-measurable), not in drug discovery.
- Scribes are commoditizing quickly: the EHR incumbent (Epic plus Microsoft) bundles the feature. Standalone vendors must move up-stack (coding, orders, revenue cycle) to survive.

## Q2: Investment flow — VC funding, big rounds, M&A, pharma-AI partnerships

### Takeaway
Capital is abundant but heavily concentrated in a few AI leaders. US digital health VC was about $11.1B year-to-date through Q3 2026 (above 2025's pace). The mega-rounds went to Isomorphic ($2.1B in May 2026), Abridge ($5.3B valuation) and similar leaders. There have been almost no US digital-health IPOs since August 2025, so exits come through M&A.

## Q4: Which business models capture value from cheaper compute vs get commoditized

### Takeaway
Value accrues to owners of proprietary clinical or multimodal data, of regulatory/reimbursement moats (FDA-cleared, CPT-coded AI), and of workflow/system-of-record lock-in (Epic, Pro Medicus, Waystar). Features that run on commodity LLMs, such as ambient documentation and generic chat tools, are being bundled by incumbents. Cheaper compute lowers their cost but also their price.

### Inferences
**Likely winners from cheaper compute:**
1. Proprietary-data owners that can sell data and models (Tempus).
2. FDA-cleared, reimbursed AI with a CPT code (HeartFlow, and imaging AI broadly). Compute is a small share of cost while price is set by reimbursement, so falling compute costs widen margins.
3. Workflow systems of record that add AI as an upsell (Pro Medicus, Waystar, Veeva, and Epic, which is private).
4. Drug-asset owners, if AI raises hit rates. The value is in the molecule, not the software.

**Likely commoditized:**
1. Standalone ambient scribes. (Abridge is private, which limits public exposure. The public read-through is to Microsoft and Epic.)
2. Generic AI copilots bundled free (Doximity).
3. Pure AI-discovery software or service vendors facing in-sourcing by pharma (SDGR's software line, CERT).

**Overall:** Cheaper compute favors businesses where revenue is tied to outcomes or reimbursement, not to compute consumption.
