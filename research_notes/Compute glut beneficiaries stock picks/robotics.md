# Robotics / Physical AI / Autonomy as a Beneficiary of Cheap, Abundant AI Compute

Research date: 2026-11-23. All figures are labeled with their period. Much 2026 data on humanoids and private funding comes from market trackers and secondary aggregators, not filings. Treat those figures as indicative and see the source-quality notes. This is research, not personal investment advice.

## Q1. Real progress 2024-2026: humanoids, robot foundation models, robotaxis, warehouse/industrial, surgical

### Takeaway
Physical AI is now shipping at scale in robotaxis (Waymo about 500k+ paid rides a week; Pony.ai at about 2,800 vehicles), in warehouse automation (Symbotic backlog about $22.5B) and in surgical robotics (Intuitive procedures +15%). Humanoids are still an early, China-dominated market of about 19k units in H1 2026, and US players are mostly in pilots (Figure in the hundreds of units, Tesla Optimus with no disclosed volumes). Robot foundation models are improving fast but have little direct commercial revenue so far.

### Inferences
- Cheap compute accelerates the **software and model layer** (simulation, VLA training, driving stacks). The binding constraints are physical: actuators, vehicle and robot manufacturing ramps (Aurora's delay, Optimus with no volumes), deployment uptime (Serve runs about 46% of its fleet daily), and regulation (Zoox's 2,500-vehicle cap).
- Robotaxis are the most "real" physical-AI revenue market after surgical and warehouse robotics. Waymo is far ahead of Tesla in miles and rides, but Waymo is not separately investable (it sits inside GOOGL).
- In humanoids, China leads on volume while the US leads on private valuations. Chinese volumes are mostly low-cost units (Unitree G1-type) going to research, education and demo use, not proven factory ROI.

## Q2. Investment flows 2024-2026: VC, mega-rounds, valuations, China vs US, government

### Takeaway
Robotics venture funding roughly doubled from 2024 to 2025 (about $7.8B to about $13.8B by one Crunchbase-cited count) and accelerated further in 2026 with $1B+ rounds. Private valuations now sit in the tens of billions (Figure $39B; Skild >$14B; Physical Intelligence ~$5.6B, reportedly seeking >$11B). China's robotics funding and IPO pipeline surged in 2026, and Unitree's STAR listing was the marquee event. Aggregate figures differ widely by source and methodology.

### Inferences
- The private-market capital has gone mostly to US model and humanoid firms (Figure, Skild, PI) at valuations far above current revenue. China has turned humanoids into listed equities faster, with Unitree and UBTech public, Hong Kong and STAR pipelines active, and euphoric retail demand. That points to bubble risk in China-listed humanoid names.
- Likely IPO and listing candidates (inferred, not confirmed): AgiBot (China; volume leader), other Chinese embodied-AI firms (Crunchbase notes "IPO momentum"), and possibly Figure or Skild later. No filed US humanoid IPO was found.

- No confirmed 2026 rounds were retrieved for Figure, 1X, Apptronik or Agility. No government program dollar amounts (US, China, Japan, Korea) were retrieved.
- Waymo's 2026 funding and valuation was not verified in this pass.

## Q3. Public stocks: large-cap quality vs early-stage high-risk, and where value accrues

### Takeaway
The best-evidenced public exposures with revenue are Intuitive Surgical (profitable compounder; growth slowing to about 14-15% procedures), Symbotic (about $22.5B backlog, turning profitable), Hesai (profitable lidar plus a fast-growing robot actuator line), Horizon Robotics (66% gross margin licensing model) and Pony.ai (fastest-scaling listed robotaxi). Tesla is an option on Optimus and robotaxi that its current numbers do not yet support. Small caps (SERV, AUR) show real but tiny revenue against large losses. Value is accruing today to **operators with scale (Waymo, Intuitive), component and sensor suppliers (Hesai, reducers), and model/compute platforms (NVIDIA)**, more than to humanoid OEMs.
