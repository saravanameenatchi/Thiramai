## Q1. Budget and policy 2025-2026 (US FY2026/FY2027, reconciliation, Golden Dome, Replicator/DAWG, CCA, drone dominance; Europe; Ukraine)

### Takeaway
US money for autonomy/drones has moved from pilot-scale (Replicator, ~$1B) to tens of billions: the FY2027 request (Apr 2026, ~$1.5T total) asks for ~$53.6B for autonomy/drone platforms/contested logistics plus ~$21B for munitions and counter-drone, ~$17.5-17.9B for Golden Dome, with much of it routed through a planned second reconciliation package — Congress has not enacted it, so this is intent, not obligated money. Europe has committed to NATO 5% of GDP by 2035 plus a €150B SAFE loan facility, which shows up already in Rheinmetall/Saab backlogs.

### Cited Findings

### Inferences
- The FY2027 numbers are a request; the autonomy surge depends heavily on a second reconciliation bill passing. A failed or shrunk reconciliation package is the single largest budget risk for drone/autonomy names.
- Drone Dominance Gauntlets are cheap, high-volume, frequently re-competed buys (~$150-300M per round). They create volume but low pricing power and fast turnover of winners (see Red Cat losing Gauntlet II). Not a moat-builder.
- CCA is a genuine program-of-record win for Anduril and General Atomics (both private). The autonomy-software layer is still open (2027 selection), with Shield AI as the independent contender.
- "Cheap compute" link: the budget explicitly funds autonomy, AI-enabled C2 and counter-UAS — workloads that scale with cheaper inference at the edge and in the cloud. The money accrues to integrators/software platforms that own fielded deployments, not to compute itself.

## Q2. Contract evidence: who wins real program-of-record money

### Takeaway
Real, large awards are concentrating in a few firms: Palantir (Army Enterprise Agreement up to $10B / 10 yrs, plus fast-growing US gov revenue), Anduril (CCA Increment 1 production, Arsenal-1 factory live), General Atomics (CCA), Kratos (Valkyrie LRIP, undisclosed customer), and European primes (Rheinmetall, Saab). Most "AI defense" small caps have small or volatile contract bases.

### Cited Findings
- Palantir Army Enterprise Agreement (announced 2025-07-31): up to $10B ceiling over 10 years, consolidates 75 contracts (15 prime + 60 related); ceiling, not obligated funds — [army.mil](https://www.army.mil/article/287506/); [The Register](https://www.theregister.com/2025/08/01/palantir_us_army_contract/)
- Palantir Q3 2026 (quarter ended 2026-09-30, reported early Nov 2026): revenue $2.313B (+96% y/y), first >$2B quarter; US government revenue ~$953M (+96% y/y, +18% q/q); US commercial $984M (+148%) overtook US government for the first time; FY2026 guide raised to $8.530-8.534B (~+91%) — [SEC 8-K exhibit 99.1](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/0001321655/000132165526000045/a2026q3ex991pressrelease.htm); [Futurum](https://futurumgroup.com/?p=97554); [SiliconANGLE](https://siliconangle.com/?p=852767)

### Inferences
- Palantir is the clearest public "AI software as program of record" beneficiary: enterprise-level agreements plus a deeply embedded C2/targeting stack (Maven). Its defense growth is real but its stock is increasingly driven by commercial AI.
- Hardware autonomy money (CCA, Arsenal-scale production) is flowing mainly to private Anduril and General Atomics; public investors get only indirect exposure (Kratos as the public CCA-adjacent/attritable aircraft play, suppliers like Karman).

## Q5. Where does value accrue: software platforms vs hardware primes vs new entrants

### Takeaway
Evidence points to a barbell: (1) software/AI platforms that become enterprise-wide systems of record (Palantir; privately Anduril Lattice, Shield AI Hivemind, Helsing) capture high margins and lock-in; (2) scaled production capacity for attritable hardware and munitions (Rheinmetall, Saab, Anduril Arsenal-1, Karman as supplier) captures volume. Small-drone hardware makers in open competitions (Gauntlets) face commoditization.

### Cited Findings
- Palantir US gov revenue +96% y/y alongside enterprise-wide Army agreement consolidating 75 contracts — [SEC 8-K](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/0001321655/000132165526000045/a2026q3ex991pressrelease.htm); [army.mil](https://www.army.mil/article/287506/)
- CCA autonomy software separately competed across six vendors (airframe ≠ autonomy), with primary autonomy provider chosen by summer 2027 — [Air & Space Forces](https://www.airandspaceforces.com/air-force-general-atomics-anduril-cca-production-contracts/)
- Drone Dominance buys tens of thousands of drones for ~$150-300M per Gauntlet across ~9-11 vendors; incumbents can lose the next round (Red Cat) — [DefenseScoop](https://defensescoop.com/2026/03/09/dod-drone-dominance-program-gauntlet-i-results/); [DroneXL](https://dronexl.co/2026/11/18/red-cat-withdraws-2026-revenue-target-gauntlet-ii/)
- VC capital concentrating in megadeals for vertically integrated new entrants (Anduril, Shield AI, Saronic, Helsing) — [PitchBook](https://pitchbook.com/news/reports/q2-2026-defense-tech-vc-first-look)
- Northrop's CCA entry lost to Anduril/GA — evidence that new entrants can beat primes on attritable platforms — [Air & Space Forces](https://www.airandspaceforces.com/air-force-general-atomics-anduril-cca-production-contracts/)

### Inferences
- Cheap compute lowers the cost of autonomy and sensor fusion, which favours (a) owners of the software layer and data/deployment footprint and (b) whoever can manufacture cheap airframes at scale. It pressures legacy primes' exquisite-platform model but they retain missiles, space and integration roles (Golden Dome).
- Public-market exposure to the strongest new entrants is limited until Anduril/Shield AI/Saronic IPO; component suppliers (Karman, Mercury-type) and Kratos are the closest listed proxies.
- Biggest risks: reconciliation/appropriations slippage; valuation compression (several names fell sharply on Q3 2026 prints); IPO supply from mega-privates; European execution delays (Rheinmetall guide cut); ceasefire scenarios in Ukraine reducing urgency.
